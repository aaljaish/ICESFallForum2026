    1,000 records · 200 encounters

    OpenAI Docs · Long-running work

    Approved MI analysis plan

    1. Validate the dataset

    Check the seven column names, binary coding, age range 18–105, income levels 1–5, 1,000 rows and 200 encounters. Tabulate missingness and repeated full rows. Retain repeated rows because coarse covariate patterns can recur. Record the independent-row and complete-follow-up assumptions. Stop with a clear error if required inputs fail validation.

    2. Explore the data

    Summarize age with mean, SD, median, quartiles and range overall and by outcome. Show an age histogram. For each categorical variable, show counts and encounter proportions by level. Inspect correlations among age and the binary covariates. Keep income categorical.

    3. Fit the prespecified model

    Use binomial logistic regression with all six covariates. Scale age per ten years. Use female, absent histories and income quintile 1 as references. Report adjusted odds ratios, 95% Wald intervals and a forest plot. Retain the prespecified covariates throughout.

    4. Check assumptions and stability

    Inspect sparse outcome cells, convergence, rank, coefficient uncertainty and extreme fitted probabilities. Report design-column VIFs. Compare linear age with a natural spline of 3 df using a likelihood ratio test and standardized probability curves. Inspect Cook’s distances and refit excluding the largest one as a sensitivity check. Report apparent AUC, Brier score and calibration by prediction decile.

    5. Interpret and deliver

    Interpret the CKD odds ratio with uncertainty and adjustment variables. Explain the distinction between odds and probability. Document the simulation design, missing patient identifiers and implications for real-data planning. Generate one HTML report with all tables and embedded figures, a plan coverage table, analysis source, input checksum and R session information. Open it to check rendering and completeness.
